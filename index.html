Floors and paint

You mentioned the floors need to be replaced throughout. Buyers notice flooring the second they walk in, and in my experience they price the fix higher than it actually costs. That's why the as-is range sits where it does.

I'd also recommend fresh paint. I suggest it for almost every listing because it makes a house feel clean and new, in photos and in person, for not much money. The two fastest sales nearby back that up:

  • 541 Newton had new carpet and fresh paint and went under contract in 2 days. It sold $10,000 over list, though the seller also paid $12,500 toward the buyer's costs.
  • 3393 Janus had fresh paint inside and out plus new carpet and went under contract in 10 days. It's still pending, so the final price isn't public yet.

What the sales tell us

  • Pricing it right the first time matters. 3059 Magnetic listed at $585,000, took 141 days to get a contract, and sold for $535,000. 3477 Janus started at $599,999, took 84 days, and sold for $560,000. Both ended up well below where they started.
  • Plan on concessions. Three of the six closed sales included seller-paid concessions: $12,500 at 541 Newton, $12,500 at 2685 Sapphire, and $5,000 at 2674 Sapphire. Every price in the ranges, the scale, and the per-foot math above is net of those concessions. When we get offers, the number that matters is what you net, not the headline price.
  • Your subdivision. The two sales in Millennium SW 18th, 2674 and 2685 Sapphire, netted $540,000 and $525,950. Both had unfinished basements and smaller lots than yours.
